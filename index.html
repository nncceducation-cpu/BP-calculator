How to interpret the result

Reference, not a treatment threshold

These centiles describe a population distribution. They do not define adequate organ perfusion and have not been validated as treatment triggers.

Measurement method matters

Use a correctly sized cuff on a settled infant. Oscillometric SBP can exceed intra-arterial SBP, especially at low pressures. MAP generally shows the closest agreement.

Evidence sources and calculator method

Numerical model: the calculator uses the Philadelphia Neonatal Blood Pressure Study reference values: birth gestation on days 0–1 (22–42 weeks), then corrected gestational age after day 1 (24–46 weeks). It reports 5th, 50th, and 95th centiles and linearly interpolates between whole-week rows; it never extrapolates.

Why the values changed: the previously displayed Dionne table was developed to identify possible hypertension and emphasizes upper centiles. It is retained below as an important hypertension source but is not used as the calculator's default normative distribution. Newer large cohorts support strong gestational- and postnatal-age effects, but do not yet provide one complete, externally validated 5th/50th/95th SBP, DBP, and MAP table across the entire neonatal age range.

Confirm on repeated measurements

A single elevated reading does not establish neonatal hypertension. Confirm measurement technique and persistence on separate occasions, then interpret with the clinical context.

Hypertension interpretation

Values at or above the 95th centile are flagged as elevated. Values at or above the 99th centile are flagged as markedly elevated. The table applies after the first two postnatal weeks and covers corrected gestational ages 26 to 44 weeks.
